# Decisions made while building

Short notes on places where the handoff, the Figma and reality disagreed, and what was done about it.

## Data

- **Met search API.** The handoff points at `/public/collection/v1/search`. That endpoint was retired on 2026-10-01 and now returns a 410 notice. Curation used `/public/collection/v1.1/search` (paginated with `limit`/`offset`). Object lookups (`/v1/objects/{id}`) still work and are what the app uses at runtime.
- **Bot protection.** The Met API sits behind Incapsula and blocks bursts of requests. `scripts/fetch-met.mjs` now throttles to about one request per second, retries with backoff, and keeps the previous `paintings.json` record when an object cannot be fetched during a run.
- **Room statements.** The handoff says to keep the existing Great Hall and European Paintings statements, but the screenshots show different copy for both rooms. The screenshot copy won, since the screens have to match at 390px. 19th-Century and American Wing statements are unchanged.
- **Room names.** The handoff table uses long names ("European Paintings, 1250–1800", "Egyptian Art and the Temple of Dendur"). The screenshots use short names in headers, breadcrumbs and door cards, so `rooms.ts` uses the short names everywhere in the UI. The long names stay in `met_floorplan.json` for map labels. The Figma door copy "The Temple of Dendur" is illustrative; the room is "Egyptian Art" and the Temple is one of its destination galleries.
- **Destination galleries.** The floor plan only has one inner gallery block (822). For paintings whose gallery is inside the chosen room's own block, the destination pin is placed at a deterministic spot inside that block (offset from the center, seeded by the object id) so the last leg of the route is always visible. Paintings in a different block (e.g. Great Hall -> Egyptian Art -> Temple of Dendur, or European Paintings -> 19th Century -> 822) route through that block.
- **Walk minutes.** Route length in meters / 80, rounded, minimum 1, plus 60 m for the Grand Staircase on floor 2 walks so a Wheat Field walk reads "about a 4 min walk" like the Figma.
- **Modern and Contemporary replaced by Greek and Roman.** The handoff lists a Modern room (galleries 900s). The Met's modern wing is closed for rebuilding, and a department-wide search found only four public-domain works from Modern and Contemporary on view anywhere (all in the Lehman wing). A 10-painting, on-view, public-domain pool for that room is impossible today. The eighth room is Greek and Roman Art instead: floor 1, `planKey: greek`, galleries 150s to 160s, right off the Great Hall, with a large Open Access pool. The room id is `greek-and-roman`; adding Modern back later is a matter of a rooms entry plus curated paintings once the wing reopens.

## Build

- **Hero painting glimpse.** The Figma uses a gold (#D4A84D) rectangle for the small painting inside the entrance doorway. It is kept as the one non-palette fill, since it stands for a painting image the way the share gradient does.
- **drei `<Html>` labels.** drei's `<Html>` drops its content under React StrictMode with React 19 (the strict double-mount unmounts the new root). The map labels are plain DOM nodes positioned every frame by projecting the anchor points, which also keeps them crisp and upright.
- **Met image CDN and CORS.** `images.metmuseum.org` answers plain image requests fine but an `<img crossorigin="anonymous">` or a first `fetch(..., { mode: "cors" })` for the same URL can come back without the CORS header (an edge-cached variant). Display images therefore load without `crossorigin`; canvas work (gradient sampling, PNG export) fetches a copy with a `?cors=1` cache-buster on the second try and degrades to the palette gradient and a card without the thumbnail if that also fails.
- **Export size.** html-to-image is given `canvasWidth: 1080, canvasHeight: 1920, pixelRatio: 1`; the card is laid out at 300x533 and scaled up by the renderer. Export takes about 2 to 4 seconds on a laptop, mostly font embedding.
- **Sign-in sheet frequency.** A guest sees the sign-in sheet the first time they tap Save in a session. After "Not now" the sheet stays out of the way for the rest of the session and Save works locally at once.
- **Offline.** The Finding screen skips the live refresh when `navigator.onLine` is false; any other fetch failure shows the error state with Retry and Try a different door.
- **Walk stats.** The card shows the real numbers: 3 rooms (hall, chosen room, destination gallery) and 6 answers. The Figma's "9 answers" assumed a three-room walk.
- **Dev shortcuts.** `?demo=<roomId>` prefills a finished walk and `?export=1` previews the exported PNG. Both are compiled out of production builds (`import.meta.env.DEV`).
- **Lint.** oxlint's `react/purity`, `react/refs` and `react/only-export-components` rules are off: they flagged `new Date()` in event handlers, refs read inside handlers, and the usual context-plus-hook module pattern.
- **npm audit.** `npm audit --omit=dev` reports a high-severity advisory in `@grpc/grpc-js`, a transitive dependency of `@firebase/firestore`. It is the Node gRPC transport; the browser build uses WebChannel and never loads it. Left as is until Firebase ships a bumped release.

## Sweep (2026-11-03)

- **Finding screen render loop.** `WalkContext` handed out new `setAnswer`/`resolve`/`reset` functions on every render, and the Finding effect depended on a callback built from `resolve`, so every step update restarted the lookup (thousands of DOM mutations a second, screen stuck on step one). The context value and its callbacks are memoized now, and the Finding screen keeps its finish handler in a ref so the effect only depends on the candidates.
- **Walk survives a reload.** Answers, chosen door, the resolved painting and the finish time are mirrored to `sessionStorage` (`mg.walk`). A tab discard on a phone or a reload mid-walk picks up where it was; starting a new walk from the entrance or the gallery clears it.
- **Stale chunk after a deploy.** `RouteMap3D` is loaded through `lazyWithRetry`: a failed dynamic import reloads the page once (so a browser holding an old `index.html` picks up the new hashes), and a second failure falls through to the new `ErrorBoundary`, which shows a plain screen with a reload link and a way back to the entrance instead of a blank page.
- **Sheet closes on text selection.** Dragging a selection out of the email field and releasing over the scrim fired a click on the scrim and closed the sheet. The scrim now closes only when the pointer went down and came up on the scrim itself.
- **Sign-in discoverability.** Guests tapping the account icon in My Gallery got a one-item menu first. The icon now opens the sign-in sheet directly for guests (signed-in users still get the menu with Sign out), and a "You are browsing as a guest" card with a Sign in button sits at the top of the gallery whenever Firebase is configured.
- **Copy link fallback.** Some embedded browsers reject `navigator.clipboard`. Copy link falls back to `execCommand('copy')` and, failing that, shows the link in a read-only field to copy by hand.
- **Map labels at the card edge.** "You are here" flips to the left of its dot when it would run off the right edge, and the gallery label is clamped inside the card.
- **Perseus and Andromeda.** The Greek and Roman room's statements cover energy, warmth and social, so outlook and focus are always zero on walks that end there; a painting whose weight sat in those axes could never reach the top three. Its mood vector now leans on the axes that room measures. A brute force over every answer combination confirms all 109 paintings are reachable.

## Walk length (2026-11-03)

- **Why.** A hall-plus-one-room walk felt like a quiz with a costume on. The walk now runs until the app is sure: at least three rooms, every mood axis asked about, no unvisited room that fits better, and one painting in the room clearly ahead of the runner-up. Thresholds (`MIN_ROOMS`, `ROOM_SLACK`, `PAINTING_MARGIN`) were tuned with a simulation over random answer patterns to a mean of 4.4 rooms with a tail to all eight.
- **One floor per map.** Walks now cross floors. Both maps still draw one floor (the floor of the last room): rooms on that floor are joined by the route line in walk order, rooms on the other floor are listed in `visited` and the stairs are lit when the walk changed floors. Minutes cover the whole walk, with 60 m per floor change.
- **Story card log.** The card has room for four stops. Longer walks show the hall, the first room, "N more rooms" and the last room.
- **Share payload.** Unchanged (`v: 1`); `stops` already carried the whole path, and `roomId` stays the last room. Old guest links still render.

## Starting room instead of Keep walking (2026-11-03)

- **Why.** Once the walk is sure, letting people wander on muddied what "found" means. The "Keep walking instead" link is gone; the doors screen sends a sure walk straight to the Final Stretch. The exploration moves to the start: the Entrance has a "Start in" selector (eight rooms in a sheet) that defaults to the Great Hall, or to the room the last finished walk ended in (`mg.lastRoom` in localStorage, written when the painting resolves). "Start a new walk" returns to the Entrance so that choice is always offered.
- **Routes from other rooms.** `routeFor` starts the route at the first room on the path; the hall waypoints and the hall block are only used when the walk began there. The Great Hall is never offered as a door, so a walk that starts elsewhere has at most seven rooms.
- **Entrance deviates from the Figma.** The screenshot shows one selector (museum). The second selector reuses the same component below it; the nearby-museums chips move down 60px.

## Selling the math (2026-11-03)

- **Bayesian core.** `src/lib/bayes.ts` replaces the heuristic stop. Mood is a Gaussian posterior per axis (prior variance 0.5, answer noise 0.35^2) and everything else is Monte Carlo over that posterior (256 samples): P(work i is the single best), P(work i is within 0.03 of the best), P(room holds a near-best work), and the expected regret R_i = E[s_max - s_i]. The walk ends after `MIN_ROOMS` when the least-regret work in the room has R_i <= 0.08 (`REGRET_OK`). Doors are ranked by P(room) + 0.6 x expected information gain from a one-step lookahead (24 imagined visitors x 64 samples over a capped candidate set). The heuristic functions stay in `matching.ts` for the simulation comparison.
- **Why regret, not "P(top work) >= 0.4".** The first rule (P(this room) >= 0.5 and P(top work) >= 0.4) worked on 109 curated works but with 1,451 works many sit within a hair of each other in mood space, so no single work ever reaches 0.4 and walks ran to all eight rooms (mean 6.8). Expected regret is indifferent to how many near-duplicates there are, and it is the same quantity the simulation scores, so the rule and the yardstick agree.
- **Thresholds by simulation.** `scripts/simulate.mts` walks synthetic visitors (six personas with correlated axes via a Cholesky factor, noise 0.3 or 0.6, 10% random answerers, 30% agree bias, 75% follow the recommended door). On the full catalog, sweeping the regret threshold: 0.04 gave 7.0 rooms, 0.06 gave 6.0, 0.08 gave 5.2 rooms with the lowest realized regret (0.195 versus 0.221 for the heuristic at 5.7 rooms), 0.10 gave 4.4 rooms but worse regret. 0.08 is the knee. About a fifth of simulated walks still visit every room: those are the random and sloppy answerers, for whom the posterior rightly never gets sharp. The realized regret (about 0.2) is far above the 0.08 the model expects, which says the noise model is optimistic about real-looking visitors; worth revisiting with actual walks.
- **Routing.** `src/lib/routing.ts` rasterizes each floor at 2 m, runs 8-connected A* with the octile heuristic and string-pulls the result. All traced blocks on both floors are connected. `routeFor` now sums real legs per floor plus 60 m per staircase.
- **Variety.** The hash-of-the-date rotation is gone. `pickWithHistory` is a penalized argmax: similarity minus 0.08 x exp(-days/7) for works in the visitor's walk history (guest localStorage or Firestore walks).
- **CLIP catalog.** `scripts/fetch-catalog.mjs` pulls on-view, public-domain, imaged objects for the six walk departments (the v1.1 search caps at 500 ids per query, so the first pass is capped per department), `fetch-images.mjs` downloads the small images, and `embed.py` (open_clip ViT-B-32, laion2b) embeds them and projects onto axis directions built from pole sentences. Raw projections are standardized per axis (median 0, 90th percentile of |x| to 1) so the CLIP vectors use the same range as the curated ones. Curated records keep their hand-written vectors and copy; CLIP records have an empty `why`, so the "Why this one" card shows only the mood lead sentence for them.
- **About page.** `/about` renders the model with KaTeX (lazy chunk) and inline SVG charts, using the live posterior when a walk is in progress. It also states what is not validated: the axes were chosen, the visitors are simulated.

## Discovered axes (2026-11-04)

- **Why.** "We picked five axes" was the weakest claim on the About page. The axes now come out of the data: Claude rated 2,946 works (every catalog image plus the 109 curated works) on 40 mood adjectives, 1 to 7, from the image with the metadata as context. Factor analysis of the standardized ratings with parallel analysis (eigenvalues against the 95th percentile of 200 shuffled copies) keeps six factors explaining 77% of the variance; varimax rotation makes each load on a small adjective set. Names: stillness (calm, serene, quiet vs restless, wild, tense), warmth (warm, convivial, content vs cold, lonely, severe), lightness (light, delicate, fresh vs heavy, sturdy, solemn), grandeur (grand, proud vs humble, plain, intimate), dreaminess (yearning, dreamy, tender vs sturdy, orderly, proud), mystery (sacred, ancient, mysterious vs worldly, fresh, convivial). The second factor is flipped so its positive pole is warm (`flip` in `scripts/llm/axis-names.json`).
- **How Claude was called.** Not the API: the `claude` CLI in print mode, the way the nestor service does it (`-p`, `--output-format json`, `--json-schema`, `--permission-mode dontAsk`, `--allowedTools Read` in an empty work folder holding only the images). Five works per call to amortize the CLI's fixed prompt, six workers in parallel, `claude-sonnet-5-5`, about 45 minutes, $64 metered on the subscription, zero refusals or bad outputs out of 590 calls. Resumable by object id.
- **Scores.** Regression factor scores, standardized per axis to [-1, 1] (median 0, 90th percentile of |x| to 1). Every work in the app has a rating, so the CLIP-to-factor ridge regression (R² 0.58 to 0.74 per axis) is only a fallback for future works. Room affinities are the mean vector of each room's works.
- **Statements.** Any number per room now. The starting room asks two per axis (12), every other room one per axis plus one more on the axis its works vary most on (7), 61 in all, in the same casual voice. Doors therefore lean on P(room) after the first room, since coverage is uniform.
- **What changed in code.** `Axis` is a string; `AXES`, pole words, tails, lead phrases, topics and labels are built from `src/data/axes.json` at load; `rooms.ts` reads affinities from `rooms-affinity.json`; `PRIOR` is built from `AXES`. Old guest share links still decode; their moods carry the old axis names, which render as neutral chips.
- **Caveat.** The rater is Claude, so the axes are structure in how a model perceives the collection, not yet in how visitors feel. The next step is unchanged: real walks, then factor analysis of the answers themselves.
